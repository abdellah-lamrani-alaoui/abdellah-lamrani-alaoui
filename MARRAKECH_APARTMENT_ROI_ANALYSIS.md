# Marrakech Apartment Investment Analysis — Best ROI in Low/Medium-Risk Neighborhoods

*Research date: June 30, 2026. Methodology mirrors the Rabat analysis in this repo: market-report ranges are treated as a starting hypothesis only — every headline yield below is checked against real, size-matched sale and rental listings from Mubawab/Avito and recomputed from scratch (gross yield = annual rent ÷ purchase price). Where the computed yield disagrees with the marketed figure, the computed one is reported as the primary number.*

## TL;DR Recommendation

**Targa** is the standout on computed, real-data yield: ~**7-9% gross**, in a residential, family-oriented, well-secured neighborhood — not the cheapest/riskiest fringe of the city. This is meaningfully higher than what real comps support for Gueliz (~5.5-7.5%) or Hivernage (~6-7%), while staying in the "good neighborhood" tier the user asked for.

**Gueliz** is the safer/more liquid alternative if you weight prestige and exit liquidity higher than the last percentage point of yield: Marrakech's actual economic and tourist center, real comps land at ~5.5-7.5% depending on furnished vs. unfurnished, plus genuine furnished/Airbnb upside (7-11% reported for well-managed short-term lets) given its built-in tourist and digital-nomad demand.

**Avoid treating Massira (or similarly cheap peripheral areas) as a primary holding.** Its nominal yield computes to a startling 9.5-13.5% — but that figure rests on a sale-price spread (420,000-700,000 DH for nominally similar 70-80 m² units, a 67% range) that's far wider than the rent spread, the same pattern that flagged Yacoub El Mansour as unreliable in the Rabat analysis. That's a sign of data noise / distressed pricing, not a real edge.

## 3. Practical strategy

1. **For the best computed risk-adjusted yield**: Targa, standard 2BR unfurnished, long-term lease to local families/professionals — ~7-9% gross, ~5-7% net after taxe d'habitation/professionnelle, syndic, vacancy, maintenance.
2. **For maximum liquidity + optionality**: Guéliz, ideally a smaller furnished unit aimed at young professionals/digital nomads or — if you're prepared to handle the regulatory and operational overhead — short-term/Airbnb rental. Confirm before buying: (a) the building's syndic permits touristic/meublé rental, (b) you can obtain the Wilaya's official tourist-rental authorization (Loi 80-14 / Décret 2-23-441, increasingly enforced as of 2026), and (c) budget 20-30% of gross short-term revenue for cleaning, management, and marketing — the 7-12% headline Airbnb yields are gross, not what lands in your pocket.
3. **Avoid Massira or similarly cheap peripheral neighborhoods as a primary holding** until you can personally inspect a handful of units — the nominal yield is unverified and the price dispersion suggests material condition or legal-status variance you can't see in listing text alone.
4. **Médina riads** are a legitimate higher-yield, higher-effort alternative if you want direct tourism exposure, but they're a different asset class (renovation capex, fire-safety compliance, full operator workload) — not a drop-in substitute for a standard apartment purchase.
5. **Due diligence**: same as Rabat — confirm clean titre foncier, syndic charges, no unresolved indivision. Marrakech's foreign-buyer-heavy market (note the EUR-denominated Hivernage listings) also means more competition and more agencies marking up prices for non-resident buyers — a local, non-agency-affiliated notary/avocat is worth the cost.

## 4. Methodology limitations (carried over from the Rabat analysis)

No photo/visual verification was possible — a headless browser was blocked by this environment's network policy from reaching Avito/Mubawab directly, and the WebFetch tool was blocked by the sites' own anti-bot protection. All matching here is text-based (size, room count, stated condition), not photo-verified. The Targa 580,000 DH comp and the entire Massira yield range are exactly the kind of result photo verification would normally resolve — flagged rather than silently included. Treat all figures as directional; verify in person or via a trusted local agent before committing capital. Listed prices are asking prices, not closing transactions — Moroccan sale prices typically negotiate 5-15% below asking.
